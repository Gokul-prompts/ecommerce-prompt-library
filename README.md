### Prompt 1: Short-form Product Description

**The Prompt:**
> Act as an expert e-commerce copywriter. Write a short-form product description for a 750ml matte black stainless steel insulated water bottle. 
> 
> Target audience: Gym-goers and office workers who are tired of lukewarm water and sweaty bottles on their desks.
> 
> Constraints:
> - Keep it under 50 words.
> - Use exactly 3 bullet points.
> - Focus on the *benefits* (staying cold, no sweat) rather than just listing the features.

**The Output:**
> Ice-Cold Refreshment: Keep your water refreshingly cold throughout workouts and busy workdays.
> No Sweaty Surfaces: Enjoy a dry bottle and a clean, sweat-free desk.
> Style Meets Convenience: Carry 750ml of hydration in a sleek, matte black stainless steel bottle.

**Why this works:**
By assigning the role of "expert e-commerce copywriter" and defining the target audience, the AI bypassed generic feature-listing. The constraint to focus on benefits resulted in pain-point-driven copy (e.g., "No Sweaty Surfaces") which is much more effective for ad conversions.

### Prompt 2: Facebook Ad Headline Variants

**The Prompt:**
> Act as a direct-response Facebook Ads copywriter. I need 5 different ad headlines for a new product: a smart notebook that instantly digitizes handwritten notes and syncs them to the cloud.
> 
> Target audience: College students and busy professionals who take messy handwritten notes but hate typing them up later.
> 
> Constraints for each headline:
> - Keep it under 10 words.
> - Do not use the words "revolutionary" or "game-changer".
> - Use a different psychological trigger for each one. Label the trigger used (e.g., Curiosity, Urgency, Social Proof, Pain Point, Benefit-driven).

**The Output:**
> 1. Pain Point: Stop Retyping Your Messy Handwritten Notes.
> 2. Benefit-driven: Write Once. Your Notes Are Digital and Searchable.
> 3. Curiosity: What Happens When Your Notebook Types for You?
> 4. Social Proof: Thousands of Students Ditched Note Typing. Here's Why.
> 5. Urgency: Finals Are Coming. Digitize Your Notes Before Then.

**Why this works:**
By explicitly requiring a different psychological trigger for each headline, the prompt prevents the AI from generating repetitive variations. This approach forces the model to explore distinct marketing angles (pain points, curiosity, social proof), giving the ad buyer a diverse A/B testing pool.

### Case Study 1: Model Evaluation & The "Urgency" Hallucination

**The Test:**
After generating Facebook ad headlines (Prompt 2), a fellow marketer asked if the AI actually kept the psychological triggers distinct, or if they blurred together. I ran the prompt 3 separate times to test this.

**The Finding:**
The AI successfully kept "Curiosity" and "Benefit-driven" distinct. However, for the "Urgency" trigger, the AI consistently hallucinated fake deadlines to create urgency (e.g., *"Order by Friday"* and *"Black Friday Deal"*), even when explicitly instructed not to mix triggers. 

**The Solution (Prompt Engineering):**
Because AI associates "Urgency" with e-commerce sales, it will invent information if not constrained. To fix this, future prompts must include a strict constraint such as: *"Do not invent deadlines, sales, or fake dates. Base urgency only on the provided product features."*

**Lesson Learned:**
Prompt engineering is not just about writing instructions; it is about **model evaluation**. You must test the AI's output to catch hallucinations and refine your constraints.

### Prompt 3: Few-Shot Instagram Caption (Style Control)

**The Prompt:**
> Write an Instagram caption for a new organic matcha green tea powder. 
> 
> Here is an example of the style I want:
> "Fuel your morning without the crash. 🍵 Just one scoop of our ceremonial-grade matcha gives you clean, jitter-free energy to crush your to-do list. #MatchaEnergy #CleanFuel"
> 
> Now, write a similar style caption for our new product, but focus on the taste (earthy and smooth) and the fact that it's sourced directly from Japan.

**The Output:**
> Earthy. Smooth. Straight from Japan. 🌿 One sip of our ceremonial-grade matcha, grown and sourced directly from Japanese tea farms, delivers a rich, velvety taste with zero bitterness. Pure flavor, from the source to your cup. #MatchaLovers #SourcedFromJapan

**Why this works:**
This demonstrates "Few-Shot Prompting." By providing the AI with just one high-quality example, the model immediately adopts the desired brand voice—using short, punchy sentences, specific emojis (🌿), and the exact hashtag format. Without the example, the AI would likely generate a long, generic paragraph.
